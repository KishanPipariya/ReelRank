## Overall Impression

The catalogue has a clear editorial identity and now provides a quicker route into browsing. The remaining friction is concentrated in the scope users infer from search and the number of decisions inside the filter and tools disclosures.

## What’s Working

- The hero explains the catalogue premise and gives visitors a direct “Browse the catalogue” action.
- Poster wall and ledger support distinct browse and comparison tasks within one visual system.
- Active-filter chips, result announcements, and recovery states make exploratory browsing easier to understand and undo.

## Priority Issues

1. **[P1] Search scope can be mistaken for the whole catalogue.** The checked-in snapshot has 27,329 total films but displays 500. “Find a film or filmmaker” may imply full-catalogue search. Clarify the scope beside search and in results; if the 500-film limit is intentional, say search covers those films. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`.
2. **[P1] Filters still reveal many unrelated decisions at once.** The Filters disclosure contains several facet categories, with more filtering available in film details. Group facets into clear categories and make groups scannable before users enter them. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`.
3. **[P2] Catalogue tools bundles distinct tasks.** Saved views, settings, export, print, and sharing sit inside one disclosure. Group these by user intent and make repeat-use actions easier to find. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`.
4. **[P2] “Watchlist in orbit” conflicts with the ranking model.** It suggests a manually curated list rather than films aggregated from liked lists. Replace it with copy about list appearances. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`.
5. **[P2] Local preference deletion has no undo.** “Delete” and “Clear local preferences” can remove saved work immediately. Add a concise confirmation or reversible undo notice. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`.

- **Jordan, First-Timer:** The hero explains the ranking idea, but search scope is easy to overestimate and the filter categories can make browsing feel harder than the hero suggests.
- **Sam, Accessibility-Dependent User:** Source includes a skip link, labeled search, shortcut, focus styling, live result announcements, and native disclosures. Rendered keyboard and screen-reader behavior could not be reviewed; the nested filter controls are the area most likely to create navigation burden.

## Minor Observations

## Questions to Consider

- Should search cover every ranked film, or should the interface clearly frame itself as a top-500 catalogue?
- Can Filters open on a short set of common choices, with less-used facets grouped separately?
- What wording best communicates that rank comes from repeated appearances in liked lists?
