## Design Specificity Verdict

**Strongly specific to ReelRank.** Warm paper, evergreen framing, film artwork, a ledger view, and list-appearance provenance create a coherent catalogue identity. The design connects the ranking to evidence users can inspect, so it feels authored for the product rather than like a generic film database.

The main opportunity is to keep catalogue exploration in front. As more filters and utility actions have accumulated, the controls can feel like the product’s main event.

## Overall Impression

The interface has a clear personality and a useful pair of viewing modes. The biggest friction is the transition from the welcoming hero to a dense tool area before users reach the films.

## What’s Working

- **Rankings are explainable.** List counts, the score key, and source links connect the ranking to its underlying lists.
- **Poster and ledger views serve different needs.** One supports visual browsing; the other supports comparison.
- **The visual language fits the product.** Paper, evergreen, serif titles, and mono metadata support the personal catalogue idea.

## Priority Issues

1. **[P1] Advanced tools crowd the filter task.** The filter panel includes facet controls and a save/share/export group, making it harder to scan when the goal is simply to narrow the catalogue. This becomes more pronounced when the panel opens inline on mobile. Keep the facets together and give saved views, sharing, exports, and settings a separate, clearly named group. The utility section begins at `web/index.html:199`. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

2. **[P1] The catalogue starts after a long introduction.** The hero, summary, watch-progress block, and section spacing all come before the catalogue controls and films. Consider reducing the vertical space above the catalogue or adding a prominent “Browse catalogue” jump link. The hero, summary, and watch progress begin at `web/index.html:46`, `:74`, and `:81`. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

3. **[P2] Facets expose many choices at once.** Genre, source-list, decade, country, and language controls can show more than four options. Search is available for some lists, but expanded indexes still demand substantial scanning. Keep large sets searchable and consider surfacing the most relevant choices first. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

4. **[P2] A large amount of interface text is small.** The detector flagged 37 functional-text instances below 11px and 8 body-text instances below 12px. Colophon labels are 7px, with other labels at 8–10px. Raise essential metadata and check both themes in a browser, especially before treating the detector’s contrast results as confirmed. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable typeset`

## Cognitive Load

**3 of 8 checklist failures — moderate load.**

- **Fails:** Single focus — controls and utility actions compete with film discovery.
- **Fails:** One thing at a time — opening the advanced area can reveal several independent decisions.
- **Fails:** Minimal choices — several facets show more than four options at once.
- **Passes:** Chunking, grouping, visual hierarchy, working-memory context, and progressive disclosure are supported by labels, sections, and disclosure controls.

Decision points with more than four visible options include genre and source-list selections, the decade strip, and expanded country and language indexes. These are natural browsing lists, but they need clear search and grouping.

## Emotional Journey

The hero and edition details create a personal welcome; the summary gives users a sense of the collection’s size. Film discovery can feel rewarding as the list-appearance ranking and its sources become visible. The emotional dip comes when users meet the dense controls before reaching the films. Empty-result recovery and retry affordances provide reassurance. Live visual inspection was unavailable, so the actual scroll length and in-browser effect could not be confirmed.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Alex, Power User:** The `/` shortcut, shareable URL state, saved views, and layout choices help. The long facet workflow and lack of an evident bulk action may slow repeated exploration.
- **Jordan, First-Timer:** “How the ranking works” and the score key explain the core signal. “Ledger,” “source lists,” and the broad advanced panel may still need explanation.
- **Casey, Distracted Mobile User:** The filter panel becomes inline on mobile, adding length before the catalogue. This can make it harder to browse one-handed or resume after an interruption.

## Minor Observations

- The `/` search shortcut is shown in the search box.
- No-results states offer actions to clear search or filters.
- The responsive fixture covers four columns on desktop and two on narrow screens, but omits the current intermediate three-column layout. It is dated 2026-10-25; CSS includes the tablet layout and was updated afterward.
